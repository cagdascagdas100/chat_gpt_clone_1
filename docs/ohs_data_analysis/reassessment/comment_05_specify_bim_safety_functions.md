# Reassessment — Comment 5: replace the generic BIM risk-management claim with explicit functions

## Reviewer comment
`Incorporate and manage risk factors böyle çok genel bir ifade`

## Anchored manuscript wording
`BIM is a widely used tool in mobile applications due to its capability to incorporate and manage risk factors, that can enabling enable early hazard identification and, while also reducing improving time and cost losses performances [18].`

## Final editorial decision
The phrase `incorporate and manage risk factors` will be removed. It does not identify what information BIM contains, what operation is performed, or what safety-management outcome is supported. The sentence is also grammatically corrupted and combines hazard identification, time performance, and cost performance without defining or evidencing those claims.

BIM will instead be described through concrete information functions documented in the surrounding literature examples:
1. associating identified hazards with specific model elements, locations, or predefined work zones;
2. visualizing where a hazard or control applies;
3. storing or organizing inspection and incident records in a structured repository;
4. linking identified hazards with corresponding preventive measures;
5. communicating location-specific safety information to users or connected monitoring systems.

The manuscript will not imply that BIM independently identifies, evaluates, controls, or `manages` risk. Those outcomes depend on the data, rules, sensors, inspection procedures, and user decisions integrated with the BIM environment.

## Approved replacement sentence
`In construction-safety applications, BIM has been used as a spatial information layer that links identified hazards to specific building elements or work zones and enables associated safety information—such as inspection or incident records and preventive measures—to be visualized, stored, and communicated [18,19,27].`

## Recommended revised paragraph opening
`In construction-safety applications, BIM has been used as a spatial information layer that links identified hazards to specific building elements or work zones and enables associated safety information—such as inspection or incident records and preventive measures—to be visualized, stored, and communicated [18,19,27]. Tariq et al. [19] used a BIM-based decision-support prototype to visualize safety information, generate reports, and maintain structured incident records in a centralized repository. Park et al. [27] linked predefined BIM hazard zones with worker-location information and cloud communication to support location-specific warnings. Aksu and Ofluoğlu [18] combined BIM with augmented reality to present fall-related preventive measures in the physical work environment.`

This paragraph structure moves from a precise synthesis statement to three distinct examples. Each cited study supports a specific function instead of being used to justify a vague umbrella claim.

## Claims to remove
- `BIM incorporates and manages risk factors`;
- `BIM reduces time and cost losses` unless a cited study reports a defined comparison, metric, and evaluation design;
- `BIM improves time and cost performance` as a general property;
- `BIM enables early hazard identification` without identifying the input data and identification process;
- any wording implying that the OSH-RA application itself currently integrates BIM.

## Citation rule
Reference `[18]` alone is not sufficient for the complete synthesis sentence because it concerns a BIM–augmented-reality fall-risk application. The broader sentence should cite `[18,19,27]`, with each reference tied to the function it actually demonstrates. If any of the three detailed study descriptions is removed during final editing, the synthesis sentence and citation set must be re-audited.

## Terminology rule
Use the following verbs only where the corresponding operation is documented:
- `link` or `associate` for hazards, controls, model elements, and locations;
- `visualize` for spatial or graphical display;
- `store`, `record`, or `organize` for repository functions;
- `communicate` or `display` for warnings and preventive guidance;
- `support` for decision-making.

Avoid the unrestricted verbs `manage`, `optimize`, `eliminate`, or `reduce` unless the object, mechanism, comparator, and measured outcome are specified.

## Recommended reviewer response
`Thank you for this comment. We agree that the phrase “incorporate and manage risk factors” was overly general and did not explain the actual role of BIM in the cited safety applications. We therefore replaced it with a function-specific description. The revised text explains that BIM serves as a spatial information layer for linking identified hazards to building elements or work zones and for visualizing, storing, and communicating associated records and preventive measures. We also removed the broad time- and cost-performance claim because it was not defined or directly supported by the cited sentence. The subsequent examples now distinguish the repository, location-monitoring, and augmented-reality functions reported in References [18], [19], and [27].`
