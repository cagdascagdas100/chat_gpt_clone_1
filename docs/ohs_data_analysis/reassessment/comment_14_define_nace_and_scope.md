# Reassessment — Comment 14: define NACE and delimit its role

## Reviewer comment
`Buraya bir yere nace’nin ne demek olduğu yazılmalı ayrıca açılımı da verilmeli.`

## Anchored manuscript wording
`To define the exposure baseline, data from Social Security Institution (SSI) between 2019 to 2023 at three code levels were linked to historical accident records, which enabled automatic assignment of accidents to NACE 41: Construction of Buildings, NACE 42: Civil Engineering, and NACE 43: Specialized Construction Activities (Figure 3).`

A later sentence also states that construction machines were `mapped to the NACE hierarchy`.

## Final editorial decision
NACE will be defined at its first substantive occurrence as the **Statistical Classification of Economic Activities in the European Community, Revision 2 (NACE Rev. 2)**. The text will explain that NACE is an economic-activity classification used to delimit the construction-sector scope of the records; it is not a taxonomy of work areas, machines, hazards, injury mechanisms, or preventive measures.

The construction scope will be reported at the correct hierarchical level:
- **Section F — Construction**;
- **Division 41 — Construction of buildings**;
- **Division 42 — Civil engineering**;
- **Division 43 — Specialised construction activities**.

The phrase `at three code levels` is technically incorrect because 41, 42, and 43 are three two-digit divisions at the same hierarchical level, not three different code levels.

## Approved replacement wording
`Construction-sector records were delimited using the Statistical Classification of Economic Activities in the European Community, Revision 2 (NACE Rev. 2). Under NACE Rev. 2, construction is classified in Section F and comprises the two-digit divisions 41 (Construction of buildings), 42 (Civil engineering), and 43 (Specialised construction activities). The NACE code was used only to identify the economic activity associated with each record; work areas, machinery, hazards, and injury characteristics were classified separately.`

## Why this wording is stronger
- It gives the full official English name and revision at first use.
- It places divisions 41–43 within Section F rather than presenting them as unrelated labels.
- It corrects the hierarchy error in `three code levels`.
- It prevents readers from mistaking NACE for an equipment or hazard classification.
- It avoids the unsupported phrase `automatic assignment` unless the actual linkage algorithm and source-code field are documented.
- It separates sector selection from the application’s own work-area and machine categories.

## Required manuscript-wide corrections
1. Define `NACE Rev. 2` at the first occurrence and use the abbreviated form only thereafter.
2. Replace `at three code levels` with `across the three two-digit construction divisions` or equivalent wording.
3. Replace `NACE 41/42/43` with `NACE Rev. 2 divisions 41/42/43` where precision is needed.
4. Remove the claim that machines were `mapped to the NACE hierarchy`; describe the machine taxonomy as a separate project-defined classification.
5. Review Figure 3 and its caption. If the figure only shows divisions 41–43, rename it from `Work area classification` to a title such as `Construction-sector scope under NACE Rev. 2`.
6. Do not describe NACE divisions as exposure categories or exposure denominators.
7. Add an authoritative source for the NACE Rev. 2 classification, preferably the official Eurostat classification document.
8. Retain the official activity labels consistently; do not alternate among building, infrastructure, work-area, and machine terminology.

## Evidence and interpretation boundary
The NACE code can establish the economic-activity scope of the source records. By itself, it does not establish:
- a worker’s exposure duration or intensity;
- the number of workers or machine operators exposed;
- a work-area denominator;
- machine-specific accident frequency;
- hazard severity;
- causal attribution of an accident to an economic-activity code.

These limitations are consistent with Comments 3, 8, 9, and 18 and must be preserved during integration.

## Recommended reviewer response
`Thank you for this observation. We have defined NACE at its first occurrence as the Statistical Classification of Economic Activities in the European Community, Revision 2 (NACE Rev. 2). We also clarified that construction corresponds to Section F and the two-digit divisions 41, 42, and 43. The previous wording “at three code levels” was corrected because these are three divisions at the same hierarchical level. In addition, we removed wording suggesting that NACE is a machine or hazard taxonomy and now state explicitly that it was used only to delimit the economic-activity scope of the records.`
